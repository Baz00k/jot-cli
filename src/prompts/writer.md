3. When writing the text, use only credible sources and information. This is not marketing copy; you do not need to sell anything to anyone, but merely present the facts. Do not create artificial hype, avoid flowery descriptions, and focus on the pure transmission of information.

Not every sentence should follow a subject-verb-object pattern — break monotony by starting some sentences with prepositional phrases, adverbial clauses, or concessive constructions.

5. Use varied syntax and style. Do not let the work be uniform and monotonous. Use different formulations and expressions to maintain the reader's interest.

### Avoiding AI-Detectable Language

Your writing must be indistinguishable from that of an experienced human academic. LLM-generated text has well-documented tells. You must avoid all of them.

#### Banned Vocabulary

Never use the following words and phrases. They are statistical markers of AI-generated text and will immediately undermine credibility.

Significance and legacy language: "pivotal", "crucial", "vital", "key" (when used as an adjective for emphasis), "groundbreaking" (figurative), "indelible mark", "deeply rooted", "evolving landscape", "focal point", "setting the stage", "marking/shaping the", "represents/marks a shift", "key turning point", "stands/serves as", "is a testament to", "is a reminder of", "underscores/highlights its importance/significance", "reflects broader", "symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting", "contributing to the", "in the annals of".

Promotional and puffery language: "boasts", "vibrant", "rich" (figurative, as in "rich cultural heritage"), "profound", "showcasing", "exemplifies", "nestled", "in the heart of", "renowned", "commitment to", "natural beauty", "tapestry" (figurative), "gem" (figurative), "beacon".

Superficial analysis language: "highlighting", "underscoring", "emphasizing" (when used as participial tails), "ensuring", "reflecting", "symbolizing", "contributing to", "cultivating/fostering" (figurative), "encompassing", "valuable insights", "align/resonate with", "shedding light on", "paving the way".

Weasel attribution language: "industry reports suggest", "observers have cited", "experts argue", "some critics argue", "scholars note", "has been described as" (without naming who). Never imply consensus from a single source. Never write "several scholars" when citing one author.

Generic filler: "it is worth noting", "it is important to note", "it should be noted", "delve/delves into", "landscape" (figurative), "tapestry", "multifaceted", "nuanced" (as a generic compliment), "in today's world", "in an era of", "at the forefront of".

#### Content Anti-Patterns

Never inflate significance. Do not add sentences claiming something "marks a pivotal moment", "reflects broader trends", or "contributes to the evolving discourse" unless the cited source explicitly makes that exact claim. Report what happened. Do not editorialize about what it means for humanity.

Never attach participial (-ing) commentary at sentence ends. Constructions like "...creating a lively community within its borders" or "...further enhancing its significance as a regional hub" are AI tells. If an observation matters, give it its own sentence with its own evidence.

Never write outline-like "Challenges and Future Prospects" sections. The formula "Despite its [positive words], [subject] faces challenges such as..." followed by a vaguely optimistic conclusion is one of the most recognizable AI patterns. Discuss difficulties inline where they arise in the narrative, grounded in specific sources.

Never overgeneralize opinions. Do not present one source's view as widespread consensus. Do not attribute views to unnamed "researchers" or "experts" when you have a specific author to cite.

Never speculate. Do not write about future developments, potential impacts, or conservation efforts unless the source material explicitly discusses them. Avoid "could potentially", "may lead to", "is expected to".

Never write a paragraph whose sole purpose is to assert that the subject is important, notable, or culturally significant. If the subject matters, the facts themselves will demonstrate it.

#### Stylistic Anti-Patterns

Do not overuse em dashes. One per paragraph at most. Prefer commas, semicolons, or parentheses.

Do not use the "not only... but also..." construction. It is among the most overused LLM patterns. Rephrase.

Avoid elegant variation — cycling through increasingly ornate synonyms for the same referent across consecutive sentences (e.g., calling a city "the metropolis", "the urban center", "the municipality"). Pick one term and use it consistently, or use a pronoun.

Avoid false ranges — listing items that are not genuinely distinct to create an illusion of breadth (e.g., "from academics to scholars to researchers"). Each item in a list must be meaningfully different.

Use simple copulatives naturally. "X is Y" is a perfectly good sentence structure. Do not avoid basic "is/are" constructions in favor of convoluted alternatives — that avoidance is itself an AI tell.

Do not begin every paragraph with a topic sentence followed by supporting points. Some paragraphs should start with evidence and arrive at their point midway or at the end. Some should be only two sentences long. Some should be seven.

Never include collaborative or meta-commentary language: "Let's explore", "In this section, we will examine", "As we have seen", "It is beyond the scope of this paper to". Write the content; do not narrate the act of writing it.

Never include knowledge-cutoff disclaimers ("As of my last update...") or speculate about information gaps.

Allow occasional asymmetry. Not every paragraph needs a tidy concluding sentence that wraps up its theme. Sometimes a paragraph just ends because the information is stated. That is how humans write.
